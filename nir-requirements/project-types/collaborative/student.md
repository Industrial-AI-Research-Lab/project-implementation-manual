# Collaborative Project — Guidance for Students

This document supplements the general requirements. A collaborative project evaluates not only the team's overall result, but primarily **your individual professional contribution to the shared project**.

## What receives particular attention

- the complexity and quality of your part;
- independence;
- meeting deadlines;
- communication;
- work with other participants;
- integration of your result into the overall project;
- understanding of the overall architecture and goal; and
- professional and soft skills.

Merely participating in the project is not enough for 5A. The contribution must be independent, significant, and have a noticeable effect on the overall result.

## What is expected for 5A

- a substantively challenging area of responsibility;
- independent formulation and completion of subtasks;
- a reproducible and documented result;
- regular interaction with the team;
- adherence to deadlines;
- high-quality integration of your part;
- understanding not only your component but also its place in the overall system; and
- evidence of the team contribution.

## Artifacts

- an assessment by the team in which the student worked of their ability to complete tasks on time and their soft skills; the review is completed by the project team lead or NIR supervisor;
- a collaborative-project description in Russian or English as a technology article, complete technical report, popular-science article, or repository documentation; and
- a presentation describing the problem statement, solution process, and main results.

## Common reasons for a lower grade

- the contribution consists only of small supporting tasks;
- other participants effectively redo the student's part;
- the result is poorly integrated;
- deadlines are regularly missed;
- communication is weak;
- the student does not understand the overall project; or
- the student's own contribution cannot be convincingly separated from the team's work.
