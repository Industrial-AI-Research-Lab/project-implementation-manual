# Collaborative Project — Guidance for Supervisors

This document supplements the general requirements. In a collaborative project, the key object of evaluation is the student's individual contribution to a real team result.

## When defining the project

The student must have a substantively significant area of responsibility defined in advance, allowing separate evaluation of:

- technical or research complexity;
- independence;
- result quality;
- meeting deadlines;
- integration; and
- impact on the overall project.

The area of responsibility must not be limited to “helping the team,” service tasks, or work whose authorship cannot be distinguished.

## What to monitor

- tasks and deadlines are recorded so that the contribution can be reconstructed;
- the student's result is integrated into the overall project;
- clear artifacts and documentation of the student's work exist;
- the student understands the overall architecture, not only local code; and
- the team can provide a substantive assessment of the professional contribution and soft skills.

## Requirements for 5A

The project requires an independent and significant contribution, regular and effective communication, adherence to deadlines, high-quality integration, and evidence of the team contribution. Formal participation is insufficient.

## Critical risks

- the contribution cannot be separated from other participants' work;
- tasks are too simple or merely supportive;
- the result is not integrated;
- delays are systematic;
- the team effectively replaces the student in completing their part; or
- the student does not understand the overall system.

## Organizational distinction

A collaborative project is tied to work with ITMO teams. Unlike a group project, the scale of the overall project does not have to equal the sum of N individual projects; the primary concern is the specific student's individual contribution to the team.
