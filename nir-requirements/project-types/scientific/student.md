# Scientific Project — Guidance for Students

This document supplements the general requirements. The main focus of a scientific project is a **new scientific result supported by evidence**.

## What distinguishes a scientific project

It is not enough to implement a method. You must formulate a specific scientific hypothesis or research question, demonstrate a limitation of existing approaches, and produce a substantive result of your own.

A 5A project is expected to include:

- a clearly defined scientific problem and a well-supported research gap;
- an explicit statement of scientific novelty;
- an independently developed method, approach, or other substantive scientific contribution;
- a strong baseline and, where possible, current state-of-the-art approaches;
- quantitative metrics and comparable evaluation conditions;
- a complete experimental design;
- an ablation study or another analysis of component contributions where applicable;
- robust and interpretable results;
- a discussion of limitations;
- reproducibility; and
- a complete scientific-paper preprint.

A project cannot receive 5A without a quantitative comparison against existing solutions.

## Specific criteria

The following receive particular attention:

- scientific novelty;
- consistency between the hypothesis or research question, method, and experiment;
- analysis of component contributions; and
- readiness of the result for publication.

## Required and expected artifacts

Expected artifacts include:

- a public repository containing the scientific solution and experiment code;
- a scientific-paper preprint using the template of the selected journal or conference to which the work is expected to be submitted within three months after the project ends;
- a presentation; and
- a short extended-abstract report.

## Common reasons for a lower grade

- the hypothesis is vague or effectively absent;
- novelty is claimed, but the difference from known methods is not demonstrated;
- the baseline is weak or incorrect;
- the experiments do not adequately support the claims;
- a complex multi-component method has no ablation study;
- the conclusions are stronger than the data support;
- the preprint reads like an implementation report rather than a scientific paper; or
- the student does not understand the methodology of their own work.

## Grade-level guide

**5A:** A complete study with a new result, comprehensive comparison, sound methodology, and a preprint that is nearly ready for submission.

**4B:** A strong study that lacks some experiments, comparisons, or breadth of validation, or whose paper is not yet fully ready.

**4C:** An original result and a basic body of evidence are present, but the novelty or experimental validation is noticeably incomplete.

**3D/3E:** Work and individual results exist, but they do not yet support a complete scientific conclusion.
