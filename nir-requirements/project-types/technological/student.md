# Technological Project — Guidance for Students

This document supplements the general requirements. The main focus of a technological project is an **independently developed technical solution of a high engineering standard**, rather than research novelty alone.

## What receives particular attention

- relevance of the technological problem;
- technical novelty;
- architecture;
- implementation and code quality;
- testing;
- reproducibility;
- quantitative comparison with existing solutions; and
- practical value.

A working system alone does not warrant 5A. You must show how the solution is better than or substantially different from alternatives using measurable characteristics.

## What is expected for 5A

- a clearly defined technological problem and constraints;
- analysis of existing solutions;
- an independently developed technical solution;
- a well-supported architecture;
- relevant metrics such as quality, latency, throughput, memory, cost, and robustness;
- a quantitative benchmark;
- testing;
- analysis of the individual components of a complex system;
- a high-quality repository with a README, launch instructions, pinned dependencies, tests, and documentation;
- a technology report; and
- a confident explanation of architectural and engineering decisions.

## Artifacts

- a public code repository;
- a description of the technological solution as a technology article or complete technical report; and
- a presentation describing the problem statement, solution process, and main results.

## Common reasons for a lower grade

- the project primarily combines ready-made components without an original technical solution;
- the architecture is superficial or poorly justified;
- the prototype works only in a demonstration scenario;
- tests, documentation, or a reproducible launch process are missing;
- there is no benchmark against alternatives;
- operational characteristics have not been measured; or
- the student cannot explain the architecture.

## Grade-level guide

**5A:** A complete working system of professional engineering quality with a measurable advantage over, or meaningful difference from, alternatives.

**4B:** A strong system whose benchmarking, testing, or optimization is not fully comprehensive.

**4C:** A working prototype with an original contribution but noticeable engineering debt or weak comparison.

**3D/3E:** An individual result can be demonstrated, but the system is limited, unstable, difficult to reproduce, or insufficiently validated.
