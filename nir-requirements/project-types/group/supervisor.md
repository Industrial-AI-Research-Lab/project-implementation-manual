# Group Project — Guidance for Supervisors

A group project is an additional format available **only upon application by a NIR supervisor** and, under the current rules, **only to first-year students**.

Students must not independently form a group to undertake this type of project. The governing materials explicitly state that students should not be told about this project type until applications from supervisors are available.

## Core scale principle

For N participants, the project must be comparable in volume and complexity to N individual projects.

It must be one complex task. Mechanically dividing a small task into N fragments is not acceptable.

The project **does not have to be divided into N formal subtopics**. Participants must instead have independent, substantively significant areas of responsibility within one topic and one integrated result.

## What must be justified before launch

- why the task requires a team of this size;
- which independent and challenging areas of responsibility belong to each participant;
- how the participants' results form a unified system or study;
- how the overall result will be measured;
- how individual authorship and contribution will be demonstrated; and
- how the project will provide a volume of work equivalent to N individual projects.

## What to monitor during the project

- the shared repository and commit history;
- an issue or task tracker, or another task log;
- allocation of areas of responsibility;
- integration points among components;
- measurable results for each participant;
- prevention of a situation in which one or two people perform most of the work; and
- documentation of the architecture and interfaces among parts.

## Required artifacts

- a shared public repository for all participants;
- a shared presentation covering the architecture and division of work;
- an individual report from each participant;
- a description of task allocation and interdependencies;
- a demonstration of the working result where applicable;
- an assessment of each participant's contribution; and
- evidence of contribution where necessary.

## Requirements for 5A

The following are all required: the scale of N individual projects, substantive individual areas of responsibility, one integrated result, quantitative comparison with alternatives, reproducibility, high-quality documentation, and every participant's ability to explain both their own part and its relationship to the others.

## Formal caps

- insufficient scale limits the grade to **4C**;
- inability to identify individual contributions limits the grade to **4C**; and
- an artificially divided small individual task cannot qualify as a strong group project.
