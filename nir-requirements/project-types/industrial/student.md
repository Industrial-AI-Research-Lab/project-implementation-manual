# Industrial Project — Guidance for Students

This document supplements the general requirements. The main focus of an industrial project is **demonstrated value for a real or realistic industrial process**.

Simply applying ML or an LLM to a practical task is not sufficient.

## What receives particular attention

- a real need of a user or organization;
- understanding of the business or production context;
- quality of the technical solution;
- comparison with the current process or existing solution;
- quantitative impact;
- potential for piloting or deployment; and
- interaction with the industrial customer.

## What is expected for 5A

- a clearly identified customer or user;
- a specific problem;
- a current process or baseline against which the solution is compared;
- a measurable impact on quality, time, resources, cost, reliability, or a business metric;
- quantitatively demonstrated improvement;
- a technically sound result;
- well-developed deployment constraints and requirements; and
- a substantive review from an industry representative confirming quality and practical value.

Saying that a solution “could be used by a company” is not enough. The project must show why the company needs it and what benefit it provides.

## Artifacts

The following are mandatory:

- a review from an industry representative assessing the quality of the solution and the potential use of its results;
- an industrial-project description in Russian or English as a technology article, complete technical report, or popular-science article; and
- a presentation describing the problem statement, solution process, and main results.

## Formal cap

If the mandatory evaluation or review from the industry partner is missing, the final grade cannot exceed **4B**.

## Common reasons for a lower grade

- the task is artificial and unrelated to a real process;
- there is no clearly identified user;
- no current process or baseline is defined;
- the impact is described qualitatively but not measured;
- the pilot or deployment is described abstractly;
- the industry review is superficial and does not confirm value; or
- the prototype is technically too limited for the claimed use.
